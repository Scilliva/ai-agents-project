# Building permit in Remerbaach

## When a permit is required

A building permit is required for any new construction, any extension, any
change to the facade, and any garden structure with a footprint of more than
ten square metres. Structures of ten square metres or less that are not
attached to the dwelling and are no higher than two and a half metres are
exempt, but must still be declared in writing to the technical service.

The ten square metre threshold is measured on the outside of the walls,
including any roof overhang greater than fifty centimetres. Two structures
built within three metres of each other are counted as one structure for the
purpose of the threshold, which is the rule most often discovered too late.

A swimming pool requires a permit regardless of its size where it is
permanent, and no permit where it is above ground and dismantled outside the
season. A fence higher than one metre eighty on a boundary with the public
road requires a permit, and any fence on a boundary with a neighbour
requires the neighbour's written agreement rather than a permit.

## When a permit is not required

Interior work that does not affect load-bearing structures, the replacement
of windows with identical windows, and ordinary maintenance do not require a
permit. Solar panels flat against the roof plane require a simplified
declaration rather than a full permit, and so do heat pumps installed on a
facade not visible from the public road.

Work in the protected village centre is treated differently and almost
nothing is exempt there, including the colour of a repainted facade. The
protected perimeter is shown on the map annexed to the communal development
plan, and the technical service will confirm whether an address falls inside
it by telephone.

## How to apply

The application is submitted in three paper copies and one digital copy to
the technical service, with the site plan, the drawings at scale, and the
energy performance certificate. Incomplete files are returned without being
examined, and the returned file does not hold a place in the queue.

The digital copy must be a single searchable PDF file. Files submitted as
photographs of drawings are refused. The commune has announced a fully
electronic procedure but has not given a date for it, so the paper copies
remain mandatory as of this version of the page.

Applications concerning a building on the inventory of protected buildings
are forwarded to the national service for an opinion, which adds between six
weeks and four months to the procedure.

## Processing time

The college of the mayor and aldermen decides within three months of a
complete file. The period is suspended while additional documents are
awaited, and it restarts in full rather than continuing where it stopped. A
decision may be appealed within forty days.

An applicant who has received no decision after three months and no request
for documents may write to ask for confirmation that the file is complete.
Silence is not an authorisation, and starting work without a decision is the
most expensive mistake in this document.

## Fees

The instruction fee is 250 euros for a dwelling, 90 euros for an extension
or a garden structure, and 25 euros for a simplified declaration. The fee is
payable on submission and is not refunded if the permit is refused.

A second application for the same project after a refusal is charged at half
the original fee where it is submitted within twelve months and addresses
the grounds of the refusal.
