# Civil status certificates in Remerbaach

## What the office issues

The civil status office issues birth certificates, marriage certificates,
death certificates, and certificates of civil partnership, for events
registered in Remerbaach. Events registered in another commune must be
requested from that commune, and the office cannot obtain them on a
resident's behalf.

The office also issues certificates of life, certificates of residence, and
legalisations of signature. A legalisation confirms that a signature was
made in the presence of the officer and says nothing about the content of
the document, a distinction that causes regular misunderstandings at the
counter.

Records held by the office begin in 1796 for births and 1804 for marriages.
Earlier records are held by the national archives and are consulted there.

## Who may request a certificate

A full copy of a birth certificate may be requested by the person concerned,
their spouse or partner, their ascendants and descendants, and their legal
representative. Anyone may request an extract without filiation, which shows
the name, the date, and the place of birth and nothing else.

A notary, an advocate, or a public authority acting in the exercise of their
functions may request a full copy on production of a written justification.
A genealogist may request records older than seventy five years without
justification.

Requests by a third party without one of these grounds are refused, and the
refusal is given in writing on request.

## Fees

A copy of a birth certificate costs 5 euros. A copy of a marriage or death
certificate costs 5 euros. An extract without filiation is issued free of
charge. A multilingual extract for use abroad costs 8 euros, and an
apostille for use in a country outside the relevant conventions is obtained
from the Ministry rather than from the commune.

Certificates required for a school enrolment, a communal service, or a
social file are issued free of charge on production of the request from the
body concerned. Residents frequently pay for a certificate they could have
obtained free, and the office will refund the fee on the same day if the
mistake is noticed.

## How to request

Requests are made in person at the civil status office, by post, or through
the communal website. Postal requests must include a copy of an identity
document and a stamped self-addressed envelope. Certificates requested by
post are sent within five working days, and requests made through the
website are sent within three.

The website form asks for the reason for the request. The reason is used
only to decide whether the certificate is free, and it is not recorded
against the person concerned.

## Marriage

A marriage is celebrated in the commune where at least one of the future
spouses has been resident for at least ten days. The file must be submitted
at least one month before the intended date, and the date is provisionally
reserved when the file is opened rather than when it is complete.

The file includes a birth certificate for each spouse issued within the last
six months, a certificate of residence, and, where a previous marriage has
been dissolved, the relevant decision. Documents issued abroad must be
accompanied by a sworn translation and, where applicable, an apostille.

Ceremonies take place on Friday afternoons and Saturday mornings. A ceremony
on another day is possible at the mayor's discretion and carries a fee of 80
euros.
