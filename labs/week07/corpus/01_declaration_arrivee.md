# Declaring your arrival in the commune of Remerbaach

## Who must declare

Every person who moves their main residence to Remerbaach must declare the
arrival in person at the Bureau de la Population. This applies to residents
of any nationality, including people moving from another Luxembourgish
commune, and it applies whether the dwelling is owned, rented, or occupied
under a company arrangement. Children under eighteen are declared by the
parent or guardian who holds the residence, and both parents must sign where
the child arrives from a different household.

A person who keeps a residence elsewhere and stays in Remerbaach for fewer
than one hundred and eighty days in the year is considered a secondary
resident. Secondary residents make a simplified declaration and are not
entered in the electoral roll. A resident who is unsure which category
applies should declare as a main resident and ask the Bureau to correct the
entry, because a missing declaration is treated more seriously than a
declaration in the wrong category.

Students who live in the commune during term time and keep their family
address elsewhere may choose either category. The choice affects eligibility
for the resident parking vignette and for the childcare voucher, and it can
be changed once per calendar year.

## Deadline

The declaration must be made within eight days of the day you take up
residence. The eight days are counted in calendar days and include weekends
and public holidays, so an arrival on a Friday leaves until the following
Saturday. Where the eighth day falls on a day the Bureau is closed, the
declaration is accepted on the next opening day without penalty.

A declaration made later than eight days is still accepted, but the Bureau
records the delay and the resident may be asked for a written explanation.
There is no fee for a late declaration. Repeated late declarations by the
same household can be reported to the Ministry, which is the only
consequence that has ever been applied in practice.

Departures follow a different rule. A resident leaving the commune declares
the departure at any time before leaving and no later than the day of the
move, and receives a departure certificate on the spot.

## Documents to bring

Bring a valid identity document or passport for every person in the
household, the rental contract or the deed of sale for the dwelling, and a
completed departure certificate if you are arriving from another
Luxembourgish commune. People arriving from outside the European Union must
also bring the residence permit issued by the Ministry, and the Bureau takes
a copy rather than the original.

Where the dwelling is sublet, the Bureau also asks for a written statement
from the main tenant confirming that the arrangement is known to the owner.
Where several unrelated adults share a dwelling, each adult declares
separately and each receives their own residence certificate.

Documents in a language other than French, German, English, or
Luxembourgish must be accompanied by a translation. The translation does not
have to be sworn for the arrival declaration, but it does for a marriage
file.

## Opening hours

The Bureau de la Population is open Monday to Friday from 08:00 to 11:45 and
from 13:30 to 16:30, and on the first Saturday of the month from 09:00 to
12:00. No appointment is needed for an arrival declaration, although the
Saturday opening is busy and the Bureau advises arriving before 11:00.

The Bureau is closed on public holidays and on the two working days between
Christmas and the new year. Closures are published on the communal website
at least two weeks in advance, and the same page carries the telephone
number for urgent civil status matters outside opening hours.

## What happens next

The Bureau issues a residence certificate on the spot. The certificate is
required for a resident parking vignette, for enrolment in the communal
school, and for the chèque-service accueil application. It is valid for
three months from the date of issue, and a fresh certificate is issued free
of charge on request.

Within four weeks the resident receives a welcome letter listing the
communal services, the waste collection calendar for the relevant street,
and the bin delivery date. Residents who have not received the letter after
five weeks should contact the Bureau, because the most common cause is a
letter box that is not labelled with the resident's name.
